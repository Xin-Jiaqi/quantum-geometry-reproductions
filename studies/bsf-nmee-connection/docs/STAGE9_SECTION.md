# Candidate subsection: stacking-dependent spin response

We consider a mirror-paired honeycomb bilayer with inequivalent A/B sites. In the layer basis its Hamiltonian is

$$
H(\mathbf k,\mathbf d)=\begin{pmatrix}h_0+h_R&T(\mathbf k,\mathbf d)\\T^\dagger(\mathbf k,\mathbf d)&h_0-h_R\end{pmatrix},\qquad
\delta\mathbf s_\parallel=B_z C(\mathbf d)\mathbf E_\parallel.
$$

Here h₀ contains nearest-neighbour hopping, a staggered onsite potential and intrinsic Kane–Mele spin–orbit coupling; hR is the Rashba term. The spin-independent interlayer matrix T includes both same- and cross-sublattice hopping, with amplitudes and phases determined by the displacement d. A weak magnetic field couples through the Zeeman term. We calculate the insulating low-frequency spin response to an electric field after preparing the magnetic field.

The two mirror partners carry opposite layer-resolved EB responses. Sliding modifies their hybridization and leaves a net spin response. Along the complete AB-to-BA segment, this net response reverses together with the electronic out-of-plane dipole. The reversal alone therefore does not distinguish spin control from ordinary polar-domain readout. A different relation emerges for small deviations q from a polar AB stacking:

$$
\Delta P_z=O(q^2),\qquad \Delta\operatorname{Tr}C=O(q^2),\qquad
\big(C_{xx}-C_{yy},\ C_{xy}+C_{yx}\big)=O(q).
$$

Threefold rotation suppresses the linear change of the scalar polarization and response trace, while allowing a linear change of the directional spin response. This is a distinction between polarization and response anisotropy, not a claim that their finite-displacement amplitudes are universally ordered. At a fixed intermediate stacking, increasing interlayer hybridization reverses the transverse spin response without reversing the dipole or closing the gap. The response consequently carries information about hybridization that is not specified by the polarization sign.

For a manuscript, the detailed hopping functions, response derivation, layer decomposition and convergence checks belong in the Supplemental Material. The present result does not yet establish structural bistability, an electric writing protocol, a material prediction, or a universal new mechanism.
