# Focused literature context (2026-11-08)

Liang et al., **Optically Probing the Asymmetric Interlayer Coupling in Rhombohedral-Stacked MoS₂ Bilayer**, PRX 12, 041005 (2022), DOI https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevX.12.041005 . Publisher main text checked, especially pp. 2–3, Fig. 1 and Eq. (1). Supplementary material not independently retrieved this round.

Relevant evidence: at K, C3 selection permits an asymmetric interlayer conduction–valence coupling. The authors distinguish its level-repulsion contribution from polarization-induced electrostatic potential and unequal layer gaps, and separate contributions using dual-gate optical spectroscopy. This is background for the present control strategy, not evidence that the same K-edge channel dominates NMEE. Its band-offset mechanism is not ours to claim as new.

Implication for our interpretation: hybridization can itself generate charge redistribution and electrostatic potential. The proposed fixed-reference interventions distinguish response pathways, not independent origins of the self-consistent interface. A smooth-potential-only model omits in-plane local fields and XC. Selected d+p hopping removal is also not exact layer decoupling.

Open NMEE question: does this interface create new signed spin-transition weight, rather than merely expose a pre-existing layer response or shift band edges? MoS₂ activation, MoSSe reversal and KM controls must be compared at this level; shared symmetry permission alone is insufficient evidence for a shared microscopic mechanism.
