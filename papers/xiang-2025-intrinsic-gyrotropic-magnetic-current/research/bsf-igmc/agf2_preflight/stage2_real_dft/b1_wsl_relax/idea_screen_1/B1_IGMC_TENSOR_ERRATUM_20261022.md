# Erratum: distinguish static magnetoelectric polarizability from the two IGMC channels

**2026-10-22 | SCIENTIFIC CLARIFICATION, NOT A CHANGE TO COMPUTED DATA**

This note supplements, rather than silently rewrites, B1_MAGNETIC_SYMMETRY_ZERO_DFT_AUDIT_20261022.md. That earlier note correctly derived a static polarization-response tensor under an explicitly assumed magnetic point group, but its use as a proxy for the complete IGMC tensor needs a strict scope label.

## 1. The physical response is channel-resolved

The Xiang et al. PRL 134, 116301 (2025) response is a current induced by an oscillating magnetic field, not simply a static polarization derivative:

\[
J_i(\omega)=\left[\sigma^C_{ij}(\omega)+\sigma^D_{ij}(\omega)\right]B_j(\omega).
\]

- The conduction channel \(\sigma^C\) is controlled by the Zeeman Berry-curvature sector and is time-reversal even in the channel-resolved nondissipative convention used by this project.
- The displacement channel \(\sigma^D\) is generated by the time derivative of a magnetic-field-induced charge polarization; it is time-reversal odd in that convention and is the channel to which a static-like magnetic polarizability is most directly related (with the appropriate frequency factor and convention).

Thus \(\alpha^{PB}=\partial P/\partial B\) is **not** the full IGMC conductivity. The allowed matrix previously derived for \(\alpha^{PB}\) matches the displacement-channel pattern under the stated assumptions, not the conduction channel.

## 2. Correct channel-resolved tensors for the conditional B1 MPG \(m'_x\)

Assume the physical Néel axis is x and the structure retains \(m_x\) glide. The screening DFT itself is non-SOC and does not determine the easy axis; this remains conditional until an SOC calculation.

For the magnetic point group \(m'_x\), the project's channel-resolved transformation convention is
\[
\sigma^C \mapsto \det(R)R\sigma^C R^\mathsf{T},\qquad
\sigma^D \mapsto \eta_T\det(R)R\sigma^D R^\mathsf{T},
\]
with \(\eta_T=-1\) for an antiunitary operation. This convention is implemented in the repository's existing src/igmc_lab/tensor_symmetry.py. The printed PRL Eq. (14) alternative is explicitly retained there; this report does not claim that the source-equation convention ambiguity has been independently closed.

The corresponding single-state allowed tensors are

\[
\sigma^C=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&c_{xy}&c_{xz}\\
c_{yx}&0&0\\
c_{zx}&0&0
\end{pmatrix},\qquad
\sigma^D=
\begin{pmatrix}
d_{xx}&0&0\\
0&d_{yy}&d_{yz}\\
0&d_{zy}&d_{zz}
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

In a 2D film with in-plane current, the most useful components are conduction \(C_{xy},C_{xz},C_{yx}\) and displacement \(D_{xx},D_{yy},D_{yz}\). Symmetry allowance is not a prediction of nonzero magnitude.

## 3. B1-to-B2 domain parity under the source-defined magnetic connectors

Let \(R_z=\mathrm{diag}(1,1,-1)\). The published stacking connection is unitary \(m_z\) for Type-I and antiunitary \(m'_z\) for Type-II. Type-II is the strained magnetic branch reported in the AgF₂ source; it must not be treated as the same zero-strain magnetic state without qualification.

Using the project's channel-resolved convention:

| Observable | Type-I: unitary \(m_z\) | Type-II: antiunitary \(m'_z\) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Structural \(P_z\) | reverses | reverses |
| Conduction \(C_{xy},C_{yx}\) | reverse | reverse |
| Conduction \(C_{xz},C_{zx}\) | same sign | same sign |
| Displacement \(D_{xx},D_{yy},D_{zz}\) | reverse | same sign |
| Displacement \(D_{yz},D_{zy}\) | same sign | reverse |
| AHE \(\sigma_{xy}\), when present | same sign | reverse |

This yields a potentially useful **crossed-parity fingerprint**: Type-I predicts \(D_{xx}\) reversal while AHE does not reverse; Type-II predicts the opposite parity relation. This is a symmetry-derived hypothesis, not yet a real-material current calculation. It must be checked against the actual SOC magnetic group, consistent laboratory axes, and the same response convention for both domains.

## 4. Why this correction strengthens the project

The defensible research question is not merely “does sliding switch \(\alpha^{PB}\)?” It is whether sliding and the magnetic connector can **separately address conduction and displacement IGMC channels**, with distinct transformation laws and measurable consequences.

The following remain open:
1. Resolve the source-level Eq. (14) versus channel-resolved symmetry convention against the paper and Supplemental Material before any final claim.
2. Establish the SOC magnetic state and actual magnetic point group for the screened AgF₂ structure.
3. Obtain a source-grounded B2 partner and verify the physical structural switching path.
4. Calculate both IGMC channels from a Wannier Hamiltonian with consistent position and spin/Zeeman operators; consider the orbital magnetic contribution separately.
5. Converge k mesh, broadening/frequency and operator gauge covariance, and report physical units.
